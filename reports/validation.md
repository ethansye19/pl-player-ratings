## 2. Position-group handling

### 2.1 How a player's group is assigned and used (code trace)

- **Assignment** (`src/ingest/positions.py`, `src/ingest/lineups.py::build_match_lineups`): the StatsBomb lineup file gives each player a list of position intervals; each interval is clipped to the player's on-pitch spans (derived from the events) and the position with the most on-pitch seconds wins (ties -> the earlier interval). Its group comes from the single table `POSITION_GROUP_BY_ID` (GK = 1; DEF = 2-8; MID = 9-16 and 18-20; FWD = 17, 21-25). The result is one `position_group` per `(match_id, player_id)` row of `lineups.parquet`; `starting_position_id` keeps the kick-off slot separately. A player therefore has one group per match, not per season.
- **Baseline** (`src/baseline/stats.py`, `rate.py`): `player_match_stats` copies `position_group` from that match's lineup rows; `raw_scores` picks the weight vector `WEIGHTS[group]` row by row. Stats are counted from the player's events regardless of the position he was in when he made them, so a player who switched role at half-time is scored entirely with his majority-role weights.
- **VAEP** (`src/vaep/rate.py`): the sum of `vaep_value` ignores groups entirely; `position_group` is copied from the lineups onto the output so that the scaler can split by it.
- **Scaler** (`src/scale/scaler.py`): `fit` builds one reference table per group from the rated rows (`minutes >= 10`); `transform` looks up a row's own group's table and raises for a group absent from the fit. No row is ever ranked against a row of another group.
- **Web export** (`src/pipeline/export_web.py`): `position_group`, `position_id` and `position` come from the lineups; ratings are joined on `(match_id, player_id)` only. The page (`web/app.js`) uses `position_id` for the pitch layout and `position_group` as a label and, since the Step 4 decisions, to show goalkeepers their baseline rating in VAEP mode (payloads unchanged).
- **Where raw scores are compared across groups**: nowhere in a rating function. The only cross-group raw comparisons are in reports (`reports/vaep_splits.md` top tables, `src/baseline/rate.py::summary` top-5 print), which are descriptive.

**None found.** All distribution rules hold for both methods on the regenerated files (1.1), rating one match alone through the public functions and the refit scalers reproduces the season files exactly (1.2), the VAEP splits are unchanged and leak-free, `raw_vaep` equals the summed action values, the web payloads equal the parquet files (1.3), and the whole test suite passes (1). ### (ii) What the decisions achieved against the original concerns A-H, and what they introduced

The 'after' numbers here are the state right after the Step 4 decisions: the current baseline and the xG-label VAEP frozen in `data/processed/xg_labels/`. Option 3 later changed the VAEP side of B(2), C and the penalties; section 8 has what it changed.

**A. VAEP goalkeepers (page shows the baseline).** Achieved: the page no longer shows the VAEP keeper rating, and the xG labels incidentally made the VAEP keeper score more meaningful: a save is now worth +0.085 on average (before -0.004); keeper saves contribute 187 of the GK total of 273 (before: carries and passes dominated, saves were negative); Spearman(baseline, VAEP) for GK rose from 0.16 to 0.44; keepers with 3+ conceded average 6.52 under VAEP (baseline 5.74). Not achieved: the VAEP keeper score still ignores goals conceded (Spearman with goal difference -0.06) - a keeper who faces many big chances and concedes two can rate 9.4 (Mignolet, 0-2 at West Ham) - and its split-half stability is 0.22 (before 0.32). Verdict: the display rule is right and should stay; the VAEP keeper number remains unfit to show on its own.

**B. VAEP shots by xG.** Achieved the stated aim: an open-play goal is now worth +0.120 to the shooter and a miss +0.020 (before +0.838 / -0.053; the builders' figures are confirmed to three decimals in 7.8), so VAEP no longer punishes a striker for shooting: the share of forward appearances with a non-positive VAEP sum fell from 49% to 33%, Fernando's 1.29-xG-no-goal match went from 4.1 to 7.7, agreement with the baseline rose from 0.45 to 0.54 (season averages 0.61 -> 0.68), and the VAEP rating became more stable overall (0.41 -> 0.52 Pearson; FWD 0.35 -> 0.62, MID likewise up). **Introduced three problems.** (1) *Goals barely count*: scoring is worth about 0.10 more than missing, a five-goal Aguero is an 8.9 (was 10.0) and outside the top 20, Huth's two headers v City are a 7.1 (was 9.9), one-goal scorers average 7.05 under VAEP (before 7.87; baseline 7.71), and 8 goal-scorers rate below 5.5 - a match rating that does not reward the goal is hard to present to fans. (2) *The outcome lottery moved to the defence*: because a state that contains a 0.5-xG shot keeps P(concede) at 0.5 whether or not the shot went in, the defending team's first action within 10 seconds of a missed shot absorbs the whole drop. In SPADL that action is a keeper save or a clearance, never a tackle: single clearances straight after missed penalties worth +0.50 and +0.54 (Steve Cook 9.9, Kouyate 9.5) top the DEF list. A separate effect looks the same on the page: one tackle that ends a dangerous dribble can be worth +0.37 (van Aanholt 9.8 in a 0-2 home defeat). Nine of the VAEP top 20 have neither goal nor assist, and DEF split-half stability fell from 0.44 to 0.29 (every other group rose). (3) *Penalties got worse*, see below. Verdict: the direction is right but the implementation over-corrects; see the options below.

**Penalties under xG labels (builders' flag i).** Confirmed: the kick is worth -0.112 to a taker who scores and -0.298 to one who misses (before +0.063 / -0.752). Mechanism, re-derived from the cached features and the fold models: the 91 penalty states carry a label of 0.813 but are predicted at 0.647 on average - 0.682 when scored and 0.497 when missed - against the fixed 0.792 prior for the state before the kick. The outcome one-hot in the state features is doing the damage (a missed penalty state looks like a failed open-play shot to the trees; 91 states cannot overrule 8,500), not just the small sample. Effect on ratings: penalty scorers average 6.28 under VAEP (before 6.92; baseline 7.51), i.e. a scored penalty now rates its taker *below* the 6.5 average, and misses average 5.60. **Not acceptable** as a published number (74 scored penalties a season, every one visibly wrong on the page). Options, cheapest first: (a) override the predicted P(scores) of a `shot_penalty` state with its label (the constant 0.7835 plus any rebound), which makes the kick worth about -0.01 whatever the outcome - a two-line change in `rate` before `formula.value`; (b) more generally use the shot's own xG as the post-shot state value for every shot type (the state after a shot is the chance that was taken), which also fixes problem B(2) at the source because the next defensive action then starts from the xG-state rather than a model guess; (c) drop the result one-hot from shot-type states or add a penalty indicator feature and retrain - slower and less certain.

**C. VAEP for defenders.** Partly achieved: own goals are now charged (own-goal scorers 4.41 under VAEP, before 6.42), and a defender's goal no longer dominates his group (one-goal defenders 7.14, before 8.52). Not achieved and partly worse: results and cards are still invisible (DEF Spearman with goal difference +0.06; red cards 6.00), full-backs still out-rate centre-backs (6.67 vs 6.43), and the clearance/tackle credit lottery (B(2)) cut DEF stability to 0.29 and put defenders from beaten teams at the top of the list. Verdict: keep the README caveat; fix B(2) before trusting VAEP defender ratings.

**D. Baseline team context halved.** Achieved: the team-context share of raw variance fell from 40% to 15% for DEF and from 13% to 4% for MID (GK unchanged at 50%, as intended); Spearman(rating, goal difference) for defenders 0.51 -> 0.37; defenders through a 4+ goal concession 5.28 -> 5.68, clean-sheet defenders 7.28 -> 7.06; split-half stability of the baseline rose (0.54 -> 0.56; DEF 0.54 -> 0.58) and agreement with VAEP for DEF rose (0.32 -> 0.43). Baseline ratings moved little (Spearman old vs new 0.969; mean |change| 0.14). Cost: busy defenders in heavy defeats rate higher (Demichelis 7.6 in the 1-3), and the knock-on effect on short substitutes below. Verdict: a clear improvement; keep.

**E. FWD weights eased, roles pooled.** Achieved in part: centre-forwards rose from 6.58 to 6.69 against wingers 6.84 -> 6.81 (gap 0.26 -> 0.12). The MID spread is unchanged (attacking midfielders 6.82 vs central 6.68) because nothing was changed there, and the 12/16 reclassification was skipped by decision. Verdict: adequate; the remaining spreads are within 0.15 and can wait.

**F. Penalties in the baseline.** Achieved: penalty scorers average 7.51 (before 7.95), one-goal scorers 7.71; `key_passes` no longer double-counts assists. Verdict: done. (The VAEP side of penalties is the open problem above.)

**G. Baseline keeper save volume** - skipped by decision; unchanged (Forster's eleven saves remain the top GK rating; GK baseline split-half still about 0.10 with 19 keepers). Acceptable to leave.

**H / short substitutes (builders' flag ii).** Confirmed: 10-20 minute baseline cameos fell from 6.01 to 5.85, with 65% now below 6.0 (before 43%); 20-45 minute appearances fell from 6.11 to 6.01. The cameos themselves did not change (median raw 0.16 -> 0.17 for MID): the starters around them did. Halving the team context lifted the bottom of every group - the 25th percentile of DEF raw scores moved from -0.06 to 0.78, the share of 60+ minute defenders with a negative raw score from 24% to 10% - so a quiet cameo that used to sit at the 30th percentile of DEF now sits at the 15th (MID 25th -> 19th, FWD 25th -> 19th). Is it acceptable? The size is small (-0.16 on the mean) but the visible effect is not: two thirds of cameos now show red on the page, which reads as 'substitutes play badly' rather than 'substitutes did little'. Options: (a) a minutes-aware offset added to the raw score inside `rate_match` (a function of the row's own minutes, so the isolation rule holds), e.g. `offset = c_group * max(0, 60 - minutes) / 60`; to return the median cameo to about the 30th percentile of its group the full offset at 0 minutes would need to be roughly DEF +0.78, MID +0.46, FWD +0.35 raw points, i.e. about one quarter of a quiet starter's score - document it in `weights.py` as the 'low activity' rule; (b) re-tune the per-action negatives that make up most of a cameo's score (`passes_incomplete` -0.05/-0.06 and `possession_lost` -0.25/-0.15: a cameo averages 1.4 incomplete passes and 0.8 possessions lost), which would move the median cameo by only about 0.1 raw and the percentile by 2-3 points - not enough on its own; (c) accept and relabel the colour bands for substitutes. (a) is the one that addresses the cause.

### (iii) Things that look right after the changes

- Every distribution rule, for both methods, on the regenerated files: means 6.500 overall and per group, 3 and 3 tens, min 3.4, max 10.0, one decimal, 10-minute rule, all 380 matches, 11 rated starters except the 6 early injuries; isolation exact; splits identical to the frozen ones and leak-free; web payloads equal the parquet files.

- The goalkeeper display rule on the page and the README caveat.

*Judgement (verifier, 2026-11-02).* Option 3 does what it was designed to do and nothing more. Penalties: the kick is now worth -0.010 to a taker who scores and -0.011 to one who misses, so penalty scorers rise from 6.28 to 6.81; but the outcome no longer matters, and players who missed a penalty average 7.06, above the scorers (baseline 7.51 and 6.52). Defending: the first non-save action after an opponent's missed shot averages +0.020 instead of +0.079 and is worth at most +0.285 instead of +0.539, no non-save action straight after a shot is worth more than +0.30 any more, and defenders' split-half stability rose from 0.294 to 0.355, still far below the baseline's 0.580. The large moves in 8.3 are of two kinds: defenders whose match rested on one clearance straight after a missed chance fall by two to three points (Charlie Daniels 9.0 -> 5.4, Paul Dummett 8.1 -> 5.0, Kouyate 9.5 -> 6.7), and players who had been charged with their own or a teammate's miss rise (Berahino 4.0 -> 6.4 after missing two penalties, James Morrison 4.8 -> 7.1, Cazorla 4.3 -> 7.1). Not fixed: goals barely count (an open-play goal +0.121 against a miss +0.021; Aguero's five goals 8.9; 75 of 870 goal-scoring appearances below 6.0, against 3 under the baseline); results and clean sheets are invisible, so Daniels' clean sheet at Leicester and Dummett's in a 1-0 win now rate 5.4 and 5.0; and one big action can still decide a rating (van Aanholt 9.6 in a 0-2 home defeat, for a tackle that ended a dribble). Verdict: option 3 is an improvement worth keeping for the experimental VAEP view; the baseline stays the headline rating.
