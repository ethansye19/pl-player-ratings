## 2. Position-group handling

### 2.1 How a player's group is assigned and used (code trace)

- **Assignment** (`src/ingest/positions.py`, `src/ingest/lineups.py::build_match_lineups`): the StatsBomb lineup file gives each player a list of position intervals; each interval is clipped to the player's on-pitch spans (derived from the events) and the position with the most on-pitch seconds wins (ties -> the earlier interval). Its group comes from the single table `POSITION_GROUP_BY_ID` (GK = 1; DEF = 2-8; MID = 9-16 and 18-20; FWD = 17, 21-25). The result is one `position_group` per `(match_id, player_id)` row of `lineups.parquet`; `starting_position_id` keeps the kick-off slot separately. A player therefore has one group per match, not per season.
- **Baseline** (`src/baseline/stats.py`, `rate.py`): `player_match_stats` copies `position_group` from that match's lineup rows; `raw_scores` picks the weight vector `WEIGHTS[group]` row by row. Stats are counted from the player's events regardless of the position he was in when he made them, so a player who switched role at half-time is scored entirely with his majority-role weights.
- **VAEP** (`src/vaep/rate.py`): the sum of `vaep_value` ignores groups entirely; `position_group` is copied from the lineups onto the output so that the scaler can split by it.
- **Scaler** (`src/scale/scaler.py`): `fit` builds one reference table per group from the rated rows (`minutes >= 10`); `transform` looks up a row's own group's table and raises for a group absent from the fit. No row is ever ranked against a row of another group.
- **Web export** (`src/pipeline/export_web.py`): `position_group`, `position_id` and `position` come from the lineups; ratings are joined on `(match_id, player_id)` only. The page (`web/app.js`) uses `position_id` for the pitch layout and `position_group` only as a label.
- **Where raw scores are compared across groups**: nowhere in a rating function. The only cross-group raw comparisons are in reports (`reports/vaep_splits.md` top-15 table, `src/baseline/rate.py::summary` top-5 print), which are descriptive.

Spearman(rating, minutes): starters 0.122, substitutes 0.043.

**Judgement.** Short substitutes are treated plausibly but not neutrally: a 10-20 minute cameo averages 6.01 (baseline) / 6.21 (VAEP) against 6.5 for the season, 43% / 28% of them rate below 6.0 and only 3.7% / 5.2% reach 7.0. Both raw scores are sums, so a quiet cameo scores near zero, which sits in the lower-middle of each group (the baseline weights file documents this choice). The compressed spread (sd about 0.45-0.5 against 0.85 for full matches) means a cameo rarely gets an extreme rating, which is the right behaviour; the mean of about 6.0-6.2 says 'a cameo in which nothing happened is slightly below average', which is defensible but is a choice the human should be aware of (a minutes-neutral treatment would need a per-group, per-minutes reference, which the contract does not provide).

## 6. Findings and recommendations

### (i) Bugs or rule violations - must fix

**None found.** All distribution rules hold for both methods when re-derived from the parquet files (section 1.1), rating one match alone through the public functions reproduces the season files exactly (1.2), the VAEP splits are leak-free and the files are mutually consistent (1.3), the web payloads match the parquet files, and the only failing tests are the 17 import failures of the unbuilt Steps 5 and 6. No code was changed by the verifier.

Two documentation nits, not code: the brief's 'Leicester City 3-1 Chelsea (14 Dec 2015)' was 2-1; and `tests/test_rules_season.py::test_each_position_group_is_anchored_separately` allows +/- 0.15 per group while CLAUDE.md promises +/- 0.1 - the data passes at +/- 0.1 (every group is 6.500), so tightening the assertion is a free improvement (a test change, hence the human's call).

### (ii) Questionable modelling choices - the human decides

**A. VAEP is not a usable goalkeeper rating.** Baseline and VAEP goalkeeper ratings correlate at rho = 0.16 (outfield groups 0.32-0.56). Goalkeepers' VAEP comes almost entirely from carries ('dribble', 31.2 of the GK total of 65.8) and passes (28.5); saves sum to -8.2 over the season (a save is worth about +0.01, a parry into danger -0.07) because the shot's own result already carries the outcome, penalty saves are worth nothing to the keeper, and goals conceded, errors and own goals are not charged at all. The raw GK score tracks the defensive value (rho 0.89), which for a keeper is credit-assignment noise: the first defending action after a dangerous attack absorbs the whole drop in P(concede) (de Gea's +0.254 carry = a 9.8 in a defeat; Guzan 8.8 with two errors and four conceded; Pantilimon 8.0 after conceding six). 6 of the ten largest disagreements are goalkeepers. Options: (1) show only the baseline for goalkeepers on the page and in explanations, with a note; (2) rate goalkeepers in VAEP from shot-related actions only (`keeper_save`, `keeper_punch`, `keeper_claim`, excluding carries and passes), which is still weak; (3) accept and document. (1) is the honest choice.

**B. VAEP is a goals-and-misses detector for attackers.** It charges every unconverted shot (mean -0.053, up to -0.54) and pays +0.84 for a goal, so the rank correlation of a forward's VAEP with his non-penalty xG is 0.07 (baseline: 0.53); 49% of forward appearances have a non-positive VAEP sum and 52% of the |value| in the FWD group comes from shots. A converted penalty is worth +0.06 to the taker (baseline: a full goal), a missed one -0.75. Consequences: 13 one-goal scorers rate below 6.0 under VAEP against 2 under the baseline (Kane 4.4 with a goal and five shots on target; Fernando 7.7 vs 4.1). This is how VAEP is defined (outcome-based labels), so it is not a bug; if the human wants a less outcome-driven VAEP, the standard fix is to value each shot at its xG-based expected value instead of its result (a change in `src/vaep`, not in weights).

**C. VAEP ranks defenders by their offensive contribution and ignores results, cards and own goals.** For DEF the raw score correlates 0.83 with offensive value and 0.46 with defensive value (defensive sd 0.079 vs offensive 0.200); a defender's goal (+0.8 to +0.98) is about four DEF standard deviations, so every top-20 VAEP defender is a goal-scorer and one-goal defenders average 8.52 under VAEP (8.01 baseline). Clearances, aerials and clean sheets have rho of about 0 with VAEP; goals conceded are not charged (DEF rho with goal difference 0.07 vs 0.51 for the baseline), an own goal costs the scorer about -0.003 and a red-card foul about -0.062 (own-goal scorers average 6.42 under VAEP vs 5.26 under the baseline; red cards 6.11 vs 4.86). Within DEF, full-backs average 6.70 against 6.40 for centre-backs under VAEP (baseline 6.53 vs 6.57). Inherent to the method; worth a caveat on the page rather than a change.

**D. The baseline's team context dominates defenders and goalkeepers.** `goals_conceded`, `clean_sheet` and `xg_faced` are identical for every teammate and explain 40% of the DEF raw-score variance and 50% of the GK variance (13% MID, 1% FWD). The baseline rating correlates 0.51 with the match goal difference for defenders and 0.44 for keepers; team mean ratings track final league points at rho = 0.90 (VAEP 0.74); winners average 6.88 and losers 6.09. A defender who plays 60+ minutes of a 4+ goal concession averages 5.28 (n = 148) whatever he did, and one with a clean sheet 7.28 (n = 873); all four Newcastle defenders got 4.3-4.9 in the 1-6 at City with -6.36 of team context between them and a goal (+3.5) unable to offset it. Whether defenders should 'share responsibility' this strongly is a design choice; if the human wants individual actions to matter more, halving the outfield team-context weights (DEF `goals_conceded` -0.8 -> -0.4, `xg_faced` -0.4 -> -0.2, `clean_sheet` 1.0 -> 0.5; MID -0.6 -> -0.3, -0.2 -> -0.1, 0.5 -> 0.25) would cut the DEF team-context share from 40% to about 14% and MID from 13% to about 4% (variance scales with the square of the weights; the individual part is uncorrelated with the team part, r = 0.04). Keep the keeper's -1.0 per goal: a keeper is directly responsible.

**E. The position groups pool roles with different profiles.** Among 60+ minute appearances, wingers (RW/LW) average 6.84 / 6.72 (baseline / VAEP) and centre-forwards 6.58 / 6.38 inside the same FWD group; attacking midfielders 6.82 against 6.63-6.67 for central midfielders in the baseline. The MID/FWD boundary depends on how StatsBomb recorded the formation (RM/LM are MID, RW/LW are FWD): 112 players appear as both and 269 of 550 players have two or more groups; a forward reclassified as a midfielder loses on average 0.16 (baseline) / 0.29 (VAEP) for the same player. The assignment itself is correct and well implemented (most minutes, one table, no leaks); the question is whether the four contract groups are the right granularity. Concrete, contract-preserving options: (1) baseline FWD weights that stop punishing the centre-forward's job - `aerials_lost` -0.10 -> -0.05 and `possession_lost` -0.20 -> -0.15 (a centre-forward loses 2.6 aerials and 3.9 possessions a match on average), `aerials_won` 0.15 -> 0.20; (2) treat Right/Left Midfield (ids 12/16) as forwards in `positions.py` (a CLAUDE.md change: 'wingers are forwards' arguably includes a 4-4-2 winger).

**F. Penalties.** The baseline counts a penalty goal as a full goal (3.0) and a miss at -2.0; VAEP gives the taker +0.06 / -0.75. Penalty scorers average 7.95 (baseline) vs 6.92 (VAEP). A 0.79-xG kick is not a 0.1-xG header; a `penalties_scored` stat weighted at about 1.0-1.5 (with `goals` then counting open-play goals only) would be more defensible than 3.0, and is an FPL-mappable stat (`penalties_missed` already exists). Also note `key_passes` includes assists, so an assist earns 2.0 + 0.5 in MID (documented, mild double count).

**G. Baseline goalkeepers reward save volume.** `saves` (+0.5) and `xg_faced` (+0.8) both grow with the number of shots a keeper faces, so a keeper behind a porous defence can rate highly (Fabianski 8.24 raw / 13 saves in a 1-2 defeat); the extremes are legitimate (Forster's 11-save 0-0 at Arsenal is the top GK rating), and keepers with 3+ conceded still average 5.74. The split-half correlation of goalkeeper baseline ratings is only 0.10 (n = 19), i.e. almost no persistent player signal; part of that is football (shot-stopping is noisy), part is the volume design. A 'goals prevented' formulation (`xg_faced - goals_conceded` at about 1.0, `saves` at 0.25) would reduce the volume effect; low priority.

**H. Short cameos sit slightly below average** (10-20 minutes: 6.01 baseline, 6.21 VAEP; see 3.4). Defensible, documented in `weights.py`; listed here only so the human decides consciously.

### (iii) Things that look right

- Every distribution rule, for both methods: means 6.500 overall and in every group, 3 and 3 ratings of 10.0 (0.03%), min 3.4, max 10.0, one decimal, 10-minute rule, all 380 matches, 11 rated starters in 754 of 760 team-matches with the 6 exceptions all early injuries.
- The isolation rule holds exactly through the public functions and the saved scalers (differences of 0.0), the VAEP folds and holdout are leak-free, `raw_vaep` equals the summed action values, and the web payloads equal the parquet files for all 10,469 player rows.
- Minutes include stoppage time (up to 103.6), substitute goalkeepers are rated as GK, the one outfield player who went in goal keeps his majority group, and the most-minutes rule resolves half-time role swaps sensibly.
- The baseline top 20 are all legitimately great performances; the five notable matches read correctly for the baseline (Mahrez, Huth, Vardy, Aguero/De Bruyne/Silva, the four Bridge scorers) and for VAEP's attackers.
- The baseline penalises what fans expect it to penalise: red cards average 4.86, own goals 5.26, errors leading to a shot 5.79, conceded penalties 5.22.
- The baseline is the more stable signal (split-half Pearson 0.54 vs 0.41; odd/even 0.60 vs 0.44), above goals + assists per 90 (0.46).
- The two methods agree moderately (Spearman 0.45 per player-match, 0.61 on season averages; MID/FWD about 0.55, DEF 0.32, GK 0.16) and their disagreements are explainable from the data every time (4.2).
- VAEP model quality claimed in `reports/vaep_splits.md` (AUC about 0.80 on never-seen games, Brier below the base rate) is consistent with the recorded metrics file and the leak-free split structure verified here; the AUC values themselves were not recomputed.
